Hi BoBers,

The end was a bit anticlimactic. How could it not be? What could live up to years of accumulated struggle and effort and longing?

It has all come back very quickly. I'm at the gym daily - a soft landing for a body used to 8+ hours of exercise a day. Driving to the supermarket. Produce gleaming, large and unnervingly regular. Before, Landcruisers meant ministers and business magnates. Here, old mate's off for a weekend camping. I don't want to forget, but it's hard to live split between two worlds.

Two things I took away.

Firstly, Africa is diverse. In language and religion, both colonial and native. In landscape: from the snow in Morocco's towering Atlas mountains to the stiflingly humid Liberian rainforest. In culture. Just in Nigeria, I experienced both the hyper-capitalist chaos of Lagos and the understated hospitality of the Muslim east during Ramadan. 

Secondly, kindness is human and universal. No matter where you go, people want to help. In more reserved places, they might need a little encouragement, but it's magical watching a closed and wary face transform instantly into a smile at the chance to lend a hand. 90% of this needs to go to these humble helpers. A million tiny nudges moving me forward. Keep an eye out - I'll be posting their stories here soon.

As expected, the trip changed me in unexpected ways. For a long while, paradoxically as an introvert, I struggled with loneliness. I got tired of the same shallow conversations, missing the deep ease and self-evidence of real friendship. I pushed myself to talk more. But the solution was actually developing this deep relationship with myself. Something that only solitude could teach me was missing.

I also learnt to better defend myself. I realised how far I'd come when, 10 minutes after a massive shouting match with corrupt porters on the boat to Equatorial Guinea from Cameroon, I felt totally calm. No jitters at all. The fight and ferocity rose instantly when needed and subsided just as quickly. I hope the continent's vibrant life never leaves my veins. Apart from the malaria.

Thanks for accompanying me on this magnificent journey - maybe time for a little one of your own?

J&BoB
