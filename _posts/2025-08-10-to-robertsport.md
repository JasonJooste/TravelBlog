Hi BoBers,

Leaving Bo for the second time was more successful. The road was pristine. Strange for the poorest country in West Africa and especially strange given the very sparse traffic. Another massive infrastructure project while the population struggles for food.

Another flat tyre. Fixed in 10 minutes. 200m down the road a loud bang. The sidewall of my tyre torn and the tube ruptured. I'd seen someone sew the sidewall together so I tried the same and it lasted another 200km or so to Monrovia! My bodgiest bodge fix so far.

The border crossing was easy and I moved right on to Robertsport, a quiet beach town in the south east of Liberia. One of the few tourist destinations in the country. I stayed at the surf club on a "platform" - a small roofed space for putting your tent - that would become my home for over two weeks. Camping during rainy season in the wettest country in Africa would've been impossible. I managed to tick surfing somewhere for a week from my trip bucket list.

I relaxed. I surfed. But, because I'm such a nerd, I spent a lot of time working on an entry for a maths exposition competition. The rain poured while I sat at my laptop. Barely two metres of visibility with mist drifting into the platform. A bit of a bizarre sight to the surfers. Honestly, I languished again, feeling disconnected and out of place in the beautiful surroundings. My attachment to technology and the internet as comfort is something I might try to look at during this trip.

I had a fever one night. I immediately knew it was malaria. Multiple friends had caught it in Sierra Leone, likely me as well. The hospital was frightening. Painfully slow, the doctor prescribed me the medication and four unrelated medications which I refused to take (deworming tablets, antibiotics, etc. - folk medicine with white coats and pills). I also paid for a second dose of medicine that the nurse took.

The sickness itself wasn't so bad, like a moderately bad flu. The real problem is when you don't get treatment. I sweated for two nights and lay in my tent. By day 3 I was back on my feet. Another day of surfing and I left for Monrovia. Happy to be on the bike again.

Beached as,

J&BoB
