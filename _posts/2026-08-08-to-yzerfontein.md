Hi BoBers,

I crossed the border slightly hungover into the beautifully desolate northern cape of South Africa. The riding was tough with corrugated gravel roads, strong headwinds and icy mist. There were few people but a lot of wildlife. Brightly coloured birds flocked and meercats rushed into their burrows as I approached. It took me a while to realise that the squeaking I was hearing wasn't BoB, but the warning calls of mice in their burrows. This part of South Africa is famous for its wildflowers and, while I caught hints of them, they'd unfortunately come early this year. 

The towns that I passed through matched the desolate landscape. Tiny beach town. Bleak and rusty. People were begging outside the supermarkets. They are sustained by tourism and mining, both of which had recently taken a hit. Peter, the owner of a guest house I stayed at, told me that the demountables were usually full of workers visiting the local diamond mine. With the massive drop in diamond prices, he had few customers and was desperately trying to scavenge what he could to keep the buildings from rusting away. Another local town, Koingnaas, hadn't had power in four weeks. People were scavenging metal from the power lines. 

Of course I came across a bunch of very kind people. Kobus and Engela paid for a hotel room and a couple of meals for me on a particularly cold day. André and Julian offered me tea and coffee and dinner at the entry post to the national park. Lizette and Dewald hosted me for a night and got me nice and tipsy on gin and tonics. Vicky gave me a coffee for free after I came back in panic after leaving my camera at her shop. Some of the generosity I've received seemed to come from a kind of solidarity among white people. I haven't quite figured out how to process that yet.

Only 3 days to go to Cape Town! My mind has naturally started to process the trip as a whole. Trying to extract any big lessons or patterns. Despite my best efforts to enjoy what's left, I'm also starting to think about what's coming next. Worrying a little about guarding this sense of hard-won peace back in the busy western life. 

Will let you know when I arrive!

Reflectively,
J&BoB
