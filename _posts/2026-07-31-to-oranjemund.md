Hi BoBers,

I encountered some magnificent scenery heading south from Maltahöhe. Wildflowers were in full bloom, coating the fields in yellow and purple. Clouds of dust left the sky so dark that I thought rain was coming.

While passing through a tiny village, I came across an enormous, rusting, mad-max construction in the middle of a field. I thought it was some kind of crazy radio communication device at first. I climbed it carefully, checking every step and saw that it was basically held together by rusty metal and prayers. Gerhard, who's taking care of a neighbouring farm, said it was made by a local farmer, an ex-engineer who dedicated himself to making a single windmill to pump all the water for his farm and spent 30 years of his life working on it. Fascinating story.

On the way, I was showered in kindness once again. Piet, the avid cyclist who delivered the pedal for me, let me stay at his campground for free and lent me a mountain bike to ride with him. I thought I was cycling fit, but I had to give up after 1.5 hours and he was gone for another 2. Darnie, who works at the campground, helped me to fix my bags and let me stay in his camper for one night as well.

Finally, I made it to Oranjemund! I stayed at a local motel with a pub that, except for the afrikaans signs, could be straight out of the aussie outback. Through a friend of a friend of a friend, I got in touch with a lovely bunch of guys: Günther, Mike and Adrien and I already feel like family in my hometown. Mike is an absolute history buff. Apparently at some point the British visited the mouth of the nearby Orange river, looking for a more convenient place than Australia to dump their prisoners. They were lied to by some local farmers about the quality of the freshwater and continued south. If this hadn't happened, Australia might have looked very different today!

This morning I visited the local museum and saw my parents in the phone books from the early 90s when I was born and the guys and I are heading down to the beach tonight for a barbie. Tomorrow I'll be crossing the border to South Africa - the final border crossing. It's going to be hard to leave.

Welcomed home,
J&BoB
