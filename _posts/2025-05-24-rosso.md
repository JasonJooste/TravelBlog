Hi BoBers,

For the second time I had the choice: Diama or Rosso. I chose Rosso, but for a different reason. While at the border, I had witnessed the appalling treatment of deported migrants by the Mauritanian authorities. I felt sorry for myself while stuck there, but it was nothing in comparison. They had their money taken and were beaten. Some didn't have documents and were stuck at the border for weeks. They had no food and had to drink dirty water. All of this during Ramadan - a sacred time of charity for Muslims. I left my water filter and gave some money to people there when I left, but it didn't feel like enough.

I decided to bring some cooking materials to some of the migrants there. Most importantly a burner, gas, some pots and cutlery. This way people would be able to cook and eat together. A chance to recover a little of their humanity. I was worried about how the Mauritanian officials would react, so I made sure to get myself stamped out first. Then I could just leave to Senegal if there were any problems. At first, I was met with confusion - they thought I wanted one of the women to cook for me. Then suspicion. Then gratitude. And finally an endless stream of stories of gross mistreatment and disdain. I chatted and listened and gave my number to some people. Only one person contacted me to ask for help immigrating to Australia. Such is life. And then the Mauritanian officials noticed and told me to leave.

All in all, I was brought back and yelled at five times, once while wrestling my passport out of my hands. Clearly what they lack in administrative ability they made up for in intimidation. Eventually, they let me leave to Senegal. I wish I could say I calmly endured, knowing that they would let me leave, but I honestly didn't know. And given the general impunity with which they acted, I was pretty scared. Luckily, I didn't take any photos at the border.

After I recovered a little, crossing the border was a breath of fresh air. The landscape was suddenly green. There were animals and life and colour in the way people dressed.

Shaken,
J&BoB
