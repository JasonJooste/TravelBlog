Hi BoBers,

I stayed with a friend-of-a-friend in Bo, an Austrian woman working at a local charity. She'd just moved in to a massive walled compound with two big houses, all pillars and marble floors. 2500 USD a *year* rent. I camped on the floor of one of the bedrooms. It was fascinating to hear her stories of working in aid and the rampant corruption. Money for accommodation for drivers who slept in their cars. Hotel rooms instead for the mistresses of the directors. Students openly sleeping with the directors on campus.

Also, of wanting to help without being a pushover. She tried to help a man with a broken leg, who then started visiting her work, demanding money for treatment, food and transport to the clinic. When he didn't get it, he didn't turn up to his appointment. Of course, massive poverty is the root cause, but it's all so desperately complex and difficult. I had a minor crisis of conscience. I'd been a little insulated to the level of need up until this point.

I also met her Leonian boyfriend who told me some gnarly stories. He turned up one day in his home town to find a fully-built house on his land - his uncle had sold it to someone without his knowledge. The legal system isn't interested in these kinds of disputes.

I fixed the broken handlebar, seat and frame bag. The new zipper broke after the first use. As I left town, I felt my back wheel wobbling. My spokes were very loose. I remember that the bike shop in Freetown said they straightened my wheel and they must not have paid attention to the spoke tension. I tried to fix it next to a hardware shop but no dice. Luckily, Hannah picked me up and brought me back to her place. I managed to rebuild the wheel myself with a makeshift rig. Wheelbuilding isn't easy, especially not your first time and especially without equipment, so I'm quite proud that I managed. I feel way more confident in rebuilding it in the future if necessary.

Finally, after more than a week in Bo I was ready to leave for the border to Liberia.

Rested and repaired,

J&BoB
