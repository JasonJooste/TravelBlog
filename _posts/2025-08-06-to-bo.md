Hi BoBers,

The Sierra Leone railway was one of the first railways in Africa, being built around the turn of the 20th century. It even included a section into the hills of Freetown, so the British colonists could live in the cool mountain air and commute to their jobs down in the city. Sadly, the line was closed in 1975.

The railway itself has long since been scavenged, but the old trail still remains with sections cut through stone and the old rail bridges repurposed for motorbike traffic. It was an absolutely stunning, muddy, rainy ride through some beautiful country, both jungle and agricultural land. The land is cleared slash-and-burn for space and for wood. There's no time or equipment to remove the stumps, so these little trip-and-stab hazards are left in the middle of the fields.

The electricity is so patchy that a business in phone charging has emerged. "Tele-centres" have solar panels and huge arrays of chargers, charging phones for about 10 cents US. I'm glad for my powerbank because cafes and restaurants generally don't have power for charging.
More days of riding and more things broken. This time a second zipper on my centre bag, a broken seat, a hole in the sidewall of my tyre punctured by a huge bit of steel on the road and patched like a tube and, of all things, a snapped handlebar. After *finally* getting a replacement front derailleur, I couldn't use it because the lever was on the snapped bar.

So I finished by limping along, wet and muddy, with no back brake or front derailleur to the next town Bo. But I was happy. It felt like real adventure again.

Trained and ready,

J&BoB
