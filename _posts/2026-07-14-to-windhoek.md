Hi BoBers,

To avoid the frankly-scary highway riding, I chose a dirt road south to Windhoek. It was isolated, corrugated and sandy - tough on both me and BoB. BoB's rack and seat supports snapped from the vibration and my knee pain flared up again.

Another challenge was finding food and water. Like Australia, Namibia is vast and sparsely populated, with distances more suited to cars than bikes. The roads are bordered by barbed-wire fences, punctuated by gates and dirt roads disappearing into the bush - farm houses well out of sight. Every hour or so a car would rush past in a cloud of dust. I managed to hail down some cars for water, but was running low on food after a couple of days. Luckily, there was a campsite with a small store and restaurant, where I wolfed down four plates of chips and a toasted sandwich.

Worried about my knee, I reluctantly continued to Windhoek on the highway. Along the way I passed luxury game and hunting resorts with electric fences, solar-powered security cameras and trucks restocking from game breeders. Seems to be big business here. All while black people walked the highways, unable to afford transport. The inequality is palpable.

In contrast, the hospitality I encountered on the way, and in Windhoek, was incredibly touching. I was offered a free stay at the Weaver's Nest campground after beers and stories with owner Günther and his son. David and Diane from South Africa invited me to join them for red wine and nachos at the next campground. Crystal and Martin - and his parents Vera and Eckie - fed me to bursting and hosted me in Windhoek. Maria and Cronje invited me for dinner and great conversation. One bike shop gave me a free second-hand seat; another called every other shop in town hunting down parts for me. I'm lucky to have met such kind people.

I was mostly occupied with repairs, but found time to visit some monuments in Windhoek. Surprisingly, a lot were built by North Korea, who've made good business building monuments and statues around Africa. They have a distinctive style - noble bronze statues and murals full of pathos and grand narratives. If you're interested, look up Mansudae Art Studio. Namibia has a bunch of their work.

Next stop is Oranjemund, where I was born!

Fresh and fit,
J&BoB
