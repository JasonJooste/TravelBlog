Hi BoBers,

Freetown was chaos. You could barely move through some streets. There was a bus lane where people laid their wares and removed them when the bus came. The throng of people compressing to the sides to allow the bus the centimetre margin to squeeze through. I got clipped by a bus. Silly white man. Not paying attention.
We rented an apartment together, looking for a haven to rest in after a few weeks of hard and rainy riding. The owner changed the price on us after we'd already booked. All standard fare for Sierra Leone.

This place was rainier than anything I've ever remotely experienced. We rode in to town and had to twice find shelters from downpours. Once for a full hour. When we decided to just ride and get wet, we watched a mini wave of water come down the mountain and push our bikes sideways, the water rising in real time. This year was a light year, apparently.

We experienced extremes. Gone were the open, trusting and trustworthy faces of rural Fouta Djallon. I was approached by many people. Many wanted money, a gift, medicine. Without as much as a hello. The literal words "give me money" were shouted at me from strangers in a variety of ways, from embarrassed to almost furious. Cash is king here. The largest note is 20 Leones, or ~80 US cents. We paid for the apartment in enormous wads of cash.

The sad thing is that the abject need behind this behaviour is obvious. There's a reason the cash denominations are so small. People can't afford to eat. They can't afford medicine. Everyone has multiple dead mothers, aunts, brothers, friends. My pleasure-and-experience-seeking presence here is in some way an affront to this constant suffering. I really struggled with this and retreated into myself a bit, spending a lot of time alone, not to anyone's benefit.

The other end of the spectrum were the comparatively-well-off cycling club who regularly refused payment for helping BoB and escorted me around the city. Truly lovely people.

All food for thought.

I spent almost two weeks in Freetown. With BoB in somewhat better shape (all parts in Freetown were scavenged second-hand), I set off for the old railway. Alone this time.

Pensively,
J&BoB
