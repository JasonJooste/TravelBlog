Hi BoBers,

I started the final stretch to Cape Town feeling extra tired and looking extra homeless. The previous day's battle had drained all my reserves, but the sight of the iconic table mountain in the distance filled the tank just enough to keep going. The destination was the Cape of Good Hope, the most south-western point of Africa. Cape Agulhas, the southernmost point of Africa, would have probably been more appropriate, but it was 400km away and I was too bloody tired. 

With 30km to the cape, I heard a giant crack and the pedals went loose - the chain had snapped. And the derailleur jockey wheel was gone. And a spoke had ripped through the rim. I bodged a fix and managed to limp ahead, with the back brake disconnected to give more space to my wobbly wheel. Fitting, given the trip's inauspicious start nearly 2 years ago.

I struggled and sweated my way to the cape, as if my body knew the end was coming. Not wanting to ride a single km more than necessary, I'd arranged for my uncle to come and pick me up. But with my phone broken I wasn't able to warn him about the delay. I arrived more than an hour late and couldn't find him. I sat and looked out at the bay, trying to process all that I'd seen and experienced - an impossible task - as a stream of tourists flowed past me to the lookout for photos. Somehow the lack of fanfare seemed fitting for a trip that had always been so deeply personal.

Eventually, I got up and found my uncle. To mark the end of the trip with an appropriate amount of suffering, I decided to carry BoB up the hundreds of steps to the lighthouse. This time I awkwardly answered a bunch of questions and even got a round of applause. But I was cooked and ready to hop in the car, have a shower and jump into bed. 

The rest of my time in Cape Town was largely uneventful. My aunt fed me relentlessly and I gained 5kg. I did a few tourist things. I reconnected with a few cyclists who'd also finished their Africa journeys. I slept a lot. Staying with family was wonderful but the sudden lack of 100% freedom grated at me.

Then I packed BoB into a box and boarded my flight. One among hundreds on their way home. 

Well fed and watered,
J&BoB
